**Killshot.** The headline totals measure what the team could name and size, not what Nvidia has at risk, and Nvidia's own 8-K of 17 November 2026, which is already in the cache, shows the gap. Nvidia paid $58.1 billion in cash for equity securities in the nine months to 25 October 2026 and added a net $46.4 billion to private stakes, which now stand at $61.65 billion. Its total unfunded equity commitments are $20 billion. The register reports "Funded $32.3 billion" (all time, at cost, including shares since sold, positions exited, and a $10 billion purchase made after the balance date) and files OpenAI's $30 billion under "Committed, not yet paid", which Nvidia's own $20 billion total makes impossible. Then 57% of "gross at least $184 billion" is one guaranty that cannot be called before fiscal 2029. A reader who checks one tile against the 8-K will stop trusting the rest.

**The next four.**
1. No credit view where the money is. OpenAI is 73.5% of gross and is graded "Insufficient information". So are 67 of 81 relationships carrying $160 billion of the $184 billion. This is a credit register without a credit read on 87% of the exposure.
2. Eleven loops are marked closed and not one has a dollar on its closing leg. The thesis that Nvidia's money returns as Nvidia revenue is asserted in every loop summary and quantified in none.
3. The register is tier 1 only by tool failure, not by choice. Every tier 2 fetch returned 403, so 54 of 81 records are one-sided, 38 of 56 equity records are lower bounds, and widely reported figures sit in the unconfirmed log. The summary does not say so.
4. "Contingent maximum" is not a maximum. CoreWeave's $6.3 billion is the order's "initial value", SharonAI's and Firmus's maxima are unknown, and Nvidia's own disclosed maxima ($118.0 billion of guarantees plus $32 billion of neocloud agreements) exceed the register's $114 billion tile. Separately, the lender of last resort verdict rests on going-concern paragraphs that conclude the companies can continue.

## Answers to the Gate R questions

**1. Is any headline number treated as exposure?** Yes, in five places. (a) `rel_coreweave_bk_2025`: the $6.3 billion "initial value" is entered as both headline and contingent_max and flows into the contingent total, gross, the BK row, the CoreWeave concentration and the loop leg (finding 2). (b) `rel_unattributed_neocloud_agreements_2026_10_25` subtracts SharonAI's "contract value of up to $4.88 billion", which the SharonAI record itself holds as headline only with contingent_max unknown (finding 16). (c) `rel_iren_lb_2026` ($3.4 billion "total contract value"), `rel_nscale_lb_2025` ($1.2 billion "aggregate amount") and `rel_kyec_pp_2026` ($1.1 billion, one sentence in an Nvidia release) set committed_total equal to the headline and are then reported as "Committed, not yet paid" (finding 1). (d) `system_view.json` `headline_binding_share` (0.598) divides gross by headline across different sets of records (finding 13). (e) `rel_amkor_pp_2026` headline is 3,000,000,000 where the source says "nearly $3 billion" (finding 27). Not at fault: the OpenAI letter of intent, Meta, Wayve and the IREN rights are correctly kept out of exposure.

**2. Is any one-sided relationship presented as reconciled?** Yes. `rel_brookfield_baiif_lp_2025` is "reconciled" although Nvidia's only words are a passing reference to a "Program" in a NAVER release and neither side gives an amount; the identical situation at Helix was downgraded to partial. `rel_coreweave_gu_2026` is "reconciled" while its own differences list says the two sides disagree on whether the framework binds. `rel_naver_eq_2026` is "reconciled" with an empty differences list although Nvidia says "plans to invest" and NAVER says "signed". `rel_openai_eqc_2025` is "reconciled" on a status (restructured) that neither side states (findings 12, 22).

**3. Would any consequence read the same for a different company?** Yes. The two consequences of `rel_motif_eqc_2026`, `rel_rlwrld_eqc_2026` and `rel_upstage_eqc_2026` are word for word identical apart from the name. Four records end "so the X stake is immaterial to Nvidia unless sized" and four end "so a lower X price reaches Nvidia only inside the unattributed non-marketable equity line". Seven insights share one sentence, five share another, and the three Crusoe, three Firmus and three Together AI records each repeat one insight. Other generic items: `rel_openai_eq_2026` first order 1, `rel_figure_eq_2025` first order 2, `rel_mistral_eq_2025` first order 1, `rel_lumentum_eq_2026` and `rel_coherent_eq_2026` second order (findings 18, 19).

**4. Do restructured or abandoned deals show their current status?** Partly. Wayve and the OpenAI letter of intent do. `rel_anysphere_eq_2025` (restructured) has an insight that never mentions the SpaceX acquisition. `rel_tusimple_eq_2023` is "completed" with an insight saying the holding is unknown, a definition saying Nvidia "had exited", a credit note saying "Position exited" and a consequence saying it "was gone a quarter later". `rel_firmus_eq_2026a` says "Nvidia owns equity" while its own consequence says the stake "may not exist". Exited positions still add $62 million to funded and gross, and the dead $100 billion letter of intent is still inside the $307 billion headline total and counted by scenario S2 as a commitment that can lapse (findings 4, 8, 12, 13, 24, 31).

**5. Is anything sourced to an excluded publication, or displayed on a single tier 2 source?** No excluded domain is in `evidence.json` (681 SEC, 198 company releases, 6 Nikkei Asia). Two problems remain. The insight on all three Firmus equity records states "withdrew its IPO while most contracted capacity is still unbuilt"; both halves rest on one Nikkei Asia article, and the second half on an analyst's opinion quoted in it (finding 4). And 69 citations in record text, most beside numbers, point to evidence ids that do not exist in `evidence.json` (finding 6). Market values come from Nvidia's 13F at 30 September 2026, not the market data class, and are 54 days old (finding 9).

**6. Does the verdict claim more than the evidence supports? Is it under 300 words?** The prose is 287 words (298 with the four function names), so it passes the length test with no room to add. It overclaims in three places and underclaims in one. "Lender of last resort: Partly supported" rests on SharonAI's going-concern paragraph, which concludes the company "is able to continue as a going concern", and on a description of Nscale that omits that its doubt was alleviated (finding 3). The overall line "not a price setter or rescuer" contradicts the function verdicts "Not testable" and "Partly supported". The evidence against liquidity, "Intel, Synopsys and Nokia were well funded, with no linked purchases", cites a governance clause, a rating downgrade and a purchase price, none of which shows either claim. The underclaim: Nvidia's own CFO commentary says customers "are growing faster than their balance sheets and long-term credit profiles can support" and that Nvidia entered the arrangements "in response". That is the most direct primary evidence for the thesis in the whole cache and it is not in the register (finding 23).
