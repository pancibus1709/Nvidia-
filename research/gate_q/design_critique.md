## Design Critique: Is Nvidia the Central Bank of AI? (site/index.html)

Gate Q design critique, 25 November 2026. Stage: final polish before publishing. Audience: investors and analysts.
Method: Playwright (Chromium), file:///home/user/Nvidia-/site/index.html, widths 1440, 1024, 768 and 390, deviceScaleFactor 1, reduced motion on and off (218 screenshots; the on and off sets are pixel-identical apart from sub-threshold anti-aliasing, so one set is cited). Screenshots cited below are in `research/gate_q/screens/`. Test scripts are in the session scratchpad (`dq/`), not in the repository.

Note on the build under test: `site/index.html` was republished during the review (80 relationships became 81, 02:20 UTC). Screenshots 01 to 26 show the earlier build; every measured test below ran on the later one. No finding depends on the difference.

Finding counts (severity-tagged): 6 Critical, 21 Moderate, 8 Minor. The Consistency table adds 12 untagged items and the Accessibility section lists 10 contrast failures, a touch-target table and screen reader gaps.

### Overall Impression

The page is calm, honest and clearly one family with the Economy Monitor: plaster ground, serif voice, color only where money moves, and a rare willingness to say "Not disclosed" and "Not computable". The biggest opportunity is that the interactive layer does not yet keep the promises the copy makes: the first sentence says every number links to its source and the headline numbers do not, filtered tiles keep unfiltered captions, the panel does not trap focus, keyboard focus on the map is off screen, the phone full-screen map has no exit, and the board-pack print cuts off half the Ledger.

### Visual Hierarchy

- **What draws the eye first**: At 1440 and 1024, the serif title, then the block of five lede paragraphs; the four 2rem tile figures come third and sit at the fold. That is the wrong order for "how much, how much is binding, where is the risk". At 390 the eye gets prose only. On the map, the eye goes to the thick cochineal OpenAI edge and the black Nvidia core with its marigold ring, which is right, and then to ten cochineal rings that are a stress artifact, which is wrong.
- **Reading flow**: Header, lede, tiles, map, Ledger, then seven more views in one scroll under a sticky nav. The flow is sensible and the nav is clear. It weakens in three places: the summary band wastes its right half at 1440; the map's own legend is the only place that says "click a node"; the Ledger's every-other-row "Full detail" strip breaks the row rhythm so the eye cannot run down a number column.
- **Emphasis**: Tile figures (2rem, weight 600, sans) are close to the weight of the 2.4rem serif title and heavier than every `h2` (1.4rem); four of them in a row are the heaviest band on the page, which the design system warns against ("below the heading's visual weight"). Suggest `.tile .v{font-size:1.75rem;font-weight:500}` and write the unit smaller: `$30.2 <span class="u">billion</span>` with `.u{font-size:.5em;font-weight:500;color:var(--ink-2)}`, which also stops the wrap at 1024 ("$107.4 / billion", 02_top_1024.png). Tile labels do not share a baseline: buttons center their content, so "Funded and still held" sits 10 px lower than "Committed" (01_top_1440.png); fix with `.tile{display:flex;flex-direction:column;justify-content:flex-start}`. In the panel the insight sentence is correctly the strongest element after the name. In Stress the result ("$3.79 billion") has no label above it; add a `.k` line "Loss in this scenario" and an Analysis label. In the verdict, "Partly supported" in ochre is the weakest text on its card though it is the conclusion.

### What Works Well

- The palette discipline is real: a calm wall, color only on money, and every deal type carries a dash pattern or label as well as a hue. It reads as the Economy Monitor's sibling.
- Honesty is designed in, not bolted on: "Not disclosed", "Not computable", "at least", the dashed "never added" headline tile, the halo for headline beyond binding, and the Stress view's opening line "These illustrate how losses would travel, not forecasts."
- The relationship panel's top block is excellent: name, badges, one serif insight sentence, four amounts with as-of dates and source chips, dates, significance with a "why" link. The citation popover (tier badge, publisher, date, short quote, link) is exactly what an analyst wants (10_panel_citation_1440.png).
- Section state persists across reloads and relationships, and the panel reopens from the URL with focus on Close.
- Script-off fallback is thought through: ranked list, inline `details` per row, server-rendered totals.
- The ranked list at 390 is a faithful text version of the map, grouped by ring, with deal types and size in words.
- Performance is far inside budget with a single self-contained file and no third-party requests; no console noise.
- Reduced motion is respected completely: no panel slide, no map fade, no smooth scroll.
- Node accessible names are well written ("OpenAI. Conditional equity, Equity, Guarantee. Gross exposure $135 billion. Press Enter to open.").
- Copy is plain and free of hype and of banned dashes; "Who else guarantees" adds proportion without drama.
