# Who guarantees whom: credit support across the AI complex

Prepared 24 November 2026 from three research sweeps (`GS_hyperscalers.md`, `GS_labs_chips.md`, `GS_neoclouds.md`). The register now holds 65 links in which a party other than Nvidia stands behind another party's lease, loan or compute contract. They involve 79 parties. Every figure is from a company filing or the company's own release. Guarantees found twice by different researchers were merged before counting.

## The finding

Nvidia is not the only supplier underwriting demand for its own products. Google, Broadcom and AMD do the same, with the same tools, and Meta does it as a buyer. Nvidia is the largest by stated maximum, but $105 billion of its $118 billion is one guaranty that cannot be called before fiscal 2029.

| Provider | What it stands behind | Largest stated figure | As of |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nvidia** | Guarantees of leases and other obligations, mostly OpenAI's rent at an Ohio campus | up to $118 billion of guarantees, of which $105 billion is not yet in force; plus up to $32 billion of neocloud agreements | 25 October 2026 |
| **Google** | Backstops of data center lease payments for other tenants: Fluidstack at four landlords, Anthropic at TeraWulf, and a future $10 billion for the Crux AI venture | $75.7 billion, of which $48.0 billion is executed | 30 September 2026 |
| **Broadcom** | Backstop and residual value support on leases of its own AI racks to one customer, matched by analysis to Anthropic | about $29 billion plus about $43 billion; plus a commitment to buy up to $42 billion of Anthropic notes issued to pay that rent | October and November 2026 |
| **Meta** | Residual value guarantees to two off-balance-sheet data center ventures (Louisiana, 80% owned by a Blue Owl affiliate; El Paso, Texas) | $28.1 billion for Louisiana; Texas not disclosed | 31 December 2025 |
| **Meta** | An obligation to buy whatever GPU capacity Nebius cannot sell, for five years | up to $15 billion | April 2026 |
| **AMD** | Lease guarantees for unnamed partners, a backstop of unsold cloud capacity, debt guarantees | $7.7 billion, $6.2 billion and $750 million; plus $13.3 billion of leases signed on its own credit for partners | 26 September 2026 |
| **AMD** | Warrants for up to 160 million AMD shares each to OpenAI and Meta, vesting as they buy AMD GPUs; up to $5 billion of Anthropic stock tied to GPU deliveries | not a payment obligation | 2025 to 2026 |
| **Amazon** | Up to $20 billion of financing for Anthropic, released as AWS delivers compute | up to $20 billion, $5 billion used | 30 September 2026 |
| **Oracle** | A guarantee of an unnamed lessor's construction borrowing | up to $3.3 billion, matured September 2026 | 31 May 2026 |
| **SoftBank, SpaceX, Amazon, OpenAI, Anthropic** | Parent guarantees of their own subsidiaries' leases (SpaceX $20.2 billion of xAI GPU leases; SoftBank about $2.9 billion; Amazon about $5.5 billion for AWS) | ordinary parent support, not third-party underwriting | 2025 to 2026 |

**Microsoft gives no guarantee found in any filing.** Its role is different: its contracts are the collateral lenders rely on. IREN's $3.6 billion of GPU loans are sized to Microsoft's roughly $9.7 billion contract, and CoreWeave and Nscale borrow against similar contracts.

## Where others stand behind the same capacity as Nvidia

Twenty-five of the 65 links sit on a company or site that also carries an Nvidia relationship.

| Company | Nvidia's support | Who else stands behind it |
|---|---|---|
| **Anthropic** | $10 billion of shares | Google backstops about $19 billion of its leases at TeraWulf. Amazon offers up to $20 billion of financing. Broadcom leases it racks and backstops the leases. AMD buys up to $5 billion of its stock. Anthropic is the most heavily supported borrower in the complex |
| **Nscale** | Shareholder, lender, lease guarantor ($860 million at Ward County, Texas) and customer | Nscale's parent guarantees rent from year six. Dell Financial Services finances its GPUs. Anthropic's up to $44.6 billion contract is its main revenue |
| **CoreWeave** | Shareholder; order to buy unsold capacity (initial value $6.3 billion) | OpenAI's contract is pledged to CoreWeave's lenders. CoreWeave guarantees its own financing vehicles. Galaxy's noteholders were shown Nvidia's order |
| **Nebius** | $2 billion warrant plus shares | Meta must buy up to $15 billion of unsold capacity: the same backstop Nvidia gives CoreWeave |
| **IREN** | $3.4 billion cloud contract; share rights | Microsoft's contract backs its GPU loans. The loan documents name Nvidia as an acceptable replacement customer if Microsoft leaves |
| **OpenAI** | $105 billion lease guaranty; $30 billion commitment | OpenAI's own parent guarantees the tenant. AMD's warrant rewards it for buying AMD GPUs |

**Ward County, Texas is the only single lease where Nvidia and another party both guarantee the rent.** Nvidia covers years one to five, up to $860 million. Nscale's parent covers year six onward. The anchor customer may step into the lease if Nscale fails.

## None of Google's capacity backstops touch Nvidia-supported capacity

Google's Fluidstack backstops (about $13 billion across TeraWulf, Abernathy, Cipher and Hut 8) have no Nvidia link in any filing. The two systems run side by side rather than on top of each other, except at Anthropic, which both support.

## What this changes

1. **The central bank verdict.** The register's test said Nvidia supplies liquidity and collateral support "from a finite balance sheet". The sweep adds that it is one of at least four suppliers doing so, which weakens the claim that Nvidia plays a unique role. This should go into the "evidence against" column for the liquidity and collateral functions. Recommended for the build.
2. **The pattern is vendor finance.** In every large case the provider is underwriting demand for its own product. Nvidia does it for its GPUs. Google does it for TPU capacity. Broadcom does it for its racks. AMD does it for its GPUs. Meta is the exception: it backstops capacity it will use itself.
3. **Anthropic is the concentration risk the map should show.** Four suppliers and two hyperscalers stand behind it at once.
